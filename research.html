Study design

Research & Impact

How the project can be tested responsibly, what evidence is needed, and how results should be communicated.

Research objectives

  • Review published evidence on retinal findings in autism and related neurodevelopmental conditions.
  • Identify accessible datasets with documented labels, acquisition methods and usage permissions.
  • Determine whether retinal and genomic records are genuinely linked and suitable for joint analysis.
  • Build and evaluate a reproducible computational workflow if data requirements are met.

Validation plan

  • Document dataset provenance, inclusion criteria and exclusions.
  • Check for duplicate images and participant overlap.
  • Split data by participant or source cohort to prevent leakage.
  • Compare against simple baselines and report uncertainty.
  • Have interpretations reviewed by qualified research mentors.

Limitations and ethics

Publicly available data may not include paired retinal and genomic records, may represent different age groups, and may have selection or image-quality biases. A model trained on one dataset may not generalize to other cameras, populations or clinical settings.

Autism is a neurodevelopmental condition, not something that can be diagnosed from an experimental website. This prototype is not a medical device and must not be used for screening, diagnosis or treatment decisions. Any future study involving people or identifiable health data must follow applicable ethics review, consent, privacy and competition rules before starting.

Starting points for references

  • SFARI Gene — curated information about genes implicated in autism research. A gene database is not a participant-level genome dataset.
  • NCBI — databases and literature resources for biomedical research.
  • National Eye Institute — retinal and vision science resources.
  • CDC Autism — public-health information and surveillance reports. Check the specific report year and population before quoting prevalence.

For the final submission, add complete citations for every paper, dataset and software resource actually used, including access dates and dataset terms.
