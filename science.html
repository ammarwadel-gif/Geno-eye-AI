Scientific background

The Science

Understanding the biological questions behind the Geno-Eye research concept.

◉

The retina and the nervous system

The retina is neural tissue that develops from the embryonic forebrain and is part of the central nervous system. Retinal imaging can therefore provide measurable information about ocular structures, but it does not automatically reveal brain function or diagnose a neurodevelopmental condition.

Research has explored retinal structure and visual function in autistic people. Findings vary across studies, so any proposed biomarker needs careful replication and age-appropriate controls.

🧬

Genomic context

Autism spectrum disorder is genetically heterogeneous. Many genes and different variant types may contribute to neurodevelopmental differences; no single gene explains all cases.

Genes such as SHANK3, MECP2 and CHD8 are examples of genes studied in neurodevelopmental research. Their inclusion in this project is a research starting point, not proof of a specific retinal signature.

Research question

Can retinal features be studied alongside genomic information?

This project investigates whether suitable, linked datasets can support analysis of relationships between retinal image features and genomic findings associated with autism research.

Important distinction: a retinal image dataset and a gene database cannot be fused into a valid genotype–phenotype model unless the records are appropriately linked at the participant or cohort level, with compatible labels and permissions.

Working hypothesis

Some neurodevelopmental genetic conditions may be associated with measurable ocular or retinal characteristics. If a sufficiently large, well-characterized linked dataset exists, computational methods may help test whether selected retinal features carry useful research signals.
